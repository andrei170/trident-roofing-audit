Some Weeks One Client, Some Weeks Ten. Your Contact Page Is Forty-One Words.

41
words on the page where somebody decides to contact you

You want a stable five or six clients a week. The swing between one and ten is the actual problem, and it is not a lead volume problem.

You have five people including yourself and a Google Ads budget already running. You are better instrumented than either competitor we checked.

But your whole site is 2,567 words across six pages, and two of those pages are almost empty.

Every page read individually, 25 September 2026
Finding 1 — Every Page, Measured
/services 781 words /privacy 721 <- your privacy policy / 704 /reviews 295 /contact 41 /gallery 25 TOTAL: 6 pages, 2,567 words
Your privacy policy is the second longest page on your website

That is not a criticism of the privacy policy. It is a measure of how little is on everything else.

The contact page is 41 words and the gallery is 25. Those are the two pages a customer visits when they have already decided they might use you — the gallery to see whether your work looks good, the contact page to work out how to reach you.

You are spending on Google Ads to bring people to a site that gives them almost nothing at the moment they are closest to buying.

Finding 2 — The Swing From One To Ten Is A Mix Problem
You told Ruchi you want a stable five or six a week, with no preference on size

A business that does one client one week and ten the next is not short of demand. It is short of predictable demand — and those are fixed by completely different things.

More advertising makes the peaks higher. It does not raise the troughs. What raises troughs is work that recurs: maintenance, inspections, landlord and agency contracts, and commercial buildings that need something doing every year whether or not anyone searched for you.

Your site does not mention any of it. There is one services page covering everything and no page aimed at the sort of customer who books you twice a year.

We would want to know what your best weeks are made of before recommending which of those to chase. If the tens are storm weeks, that is a different plan from if they are contract weeks.

Finding 3 — No Gallery Is Costing You More Than You Think
/gallery 25 words /reviews 295 words
Roofing is bought on evidence, and evidence is photographs

Nobody can assess a roof from the ground. A homeowner deciding between three quotes is looking for proof that the firm has done this before and the result looked right.

You have a gallery page with 25 words on it. Whatever is on it, there is almost nothing supporting it — no description of the job, no town, no before and after.

Your five-person team completes work every week. Photographing it costs nothing and it is the single most persuasive material any roofer can publish.

Finding 4 — Your Sitemap Declares One Page
Pages reachable from your own menu: 6 Pages declared in your sitemap: 1
Small, and worth fixing while somebody is in there

A sitemap tells search engines what exists. Yours lists the homepage and nothing else.

Search engines can still find pages by following links, so this is not fatal. But there is no reason to make them work for it, and it is a one-off fix.

What We Would Do, In Order
  • Rebuild the contact page. 41 words is the cheapest fix on this list and it sits at the end of every pound you spend on Google Ads.
  • Build the gallery properly — job, town, before and after. Your team finishes work every week and photographs cost nothing.
  • Split the services page. One page covering everything ranks for nothing in particular.
  • Add a page for the recurring work — landlords, agencies, commercial maintenance. That is what turns a one-client week into a five-client week.
  • Add the Meta pixel through the tag manager you already have, and fix the sitemap.
  • Then review the Google Ads — once the pages the clicks land on are worth landing on.

Five people, a live Google Ads budget, and a forty-one word contact page.

You have better equipment than your competitors. There is just almost nothing plugged into it.
