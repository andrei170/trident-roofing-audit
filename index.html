Your Website Went Off Between Our First Look And Today. Everything Else In This Document Is Now Secondary.

Prepared for William Stokes · AG1 Consulting

On 25 September we measured six working pages. Today every one of them says the site is offline.

Anybody who looks you up right now gets a notice telling them the website is inactive, with a support link that belongs to somebody else.

We rewrote this document because the old one is no longer true. It argued about word counts on pages that do not currently exist.

This website is currently offline. If you are the owner, please contact us. Contact Support"
This is a switch, not a crash

The domain resolves, the certificate is valid, the server answers normally and your sitemap and robots file both still load. Nothing is broken in the technical sense.

Somebody has turned the site off. That pattern, with a tidy branded notice and a “contact support” link, is what a hosting provider or website builder shows when an account lapses rather than what a hacked or failed server looks like.

We do not know the reason and we are not going to guess at it. But it is almost certainly one phone call to whoever hosts it, and that call is worth making before anything else on this page.

Finding 1 — It Is Telling Google This Is Your Website, Not That It Is Temporarily Down
WHAT THE SERVER RETURNS Every page: HTTP 200 -- "here is the page, all is well" WHAT IT SHOULD RETURN WHILE A SITE IS OFF HTTP 503 -- "temporarily unavailable, come back later" WHY IT MATTERS A 503 tells a search engine to hold your rankings and check again. A 200 tells it that a 25-word notice saying "Website Inactive" IS your website, and to index it as such. Your sitemap is still live and still lists all six pages, so it is actively inviting them to come and read it.
This is the part that does lasting damage

A site that is off for a week and returns the right code loses almost nothing. Search engines are built for that.

A site that is off and returns a 200 is quietly replacing every page it has with the same short notice, as far as a search engine can tell. That is the version that takes months to recover from.

So the urgency is not really about visitors. It is that every day this stays as it is, the position you built is being handed back.

The good news inside the bad news

The site has not been deleted. The title tag still carries your business name and Birmingham, the tracking is still embedded, and the sitemap still lists every page. It is all sitting there behind whatever has been switched off.

This is very probably a billing or account problem rather than a rebuild. Which means the first move costs nothing.

Finding 3 — The Weaknesses From Before Are Still Worth Knowing
FROM THE 25 SEPTEMBER READING /contact 41 words a contact page with nothing on it /gallery 25 words a roofer's gallery, with 25 words /privacy 721 words the second longest page on the site More words in the legal small print than in the contact page and the gallery put together, twelve times over. Sitemap listed 1 entry at the time of the first read
For a roofer the gallery is the sale

Nobody chooses a roofer on prose. They look at photographs of finished roofs and decide whether yours look like the one they want. Twenty five words is not a gallery, it is a heading.

These are worth fixing when the site comes back, and they are the difference between a site that exists and one that earns. But they are second in the queue behind getting it switched on.

What We Would Do, In Order
  • Ring whoever hosts or built the site today. Find out why it is inactive. That is free and it does not need us.
  • While it is off, get it returning a 503 rather than a 200. Whoever administers it can do that in minutes and it protects the rankings you have already paid for.
  • Then decide whether it comes back as it was or comes back better. The tracking and the structure are sound, so this is not a rebuild from nothing.
  • Fill the gallery. You are a roofer with twenty five words where the photographs should be.
  • Give the contact page something to say, beyond forty one words.
The honest question for our call

Did you know it was off? If you did, the conversation is about what to do next. If you did not, that is worth knowing in itself, because it means nothing is watching a thing your customers use to find you.

Five days ago you had the best-equipped roofing website we measured in Birmingham.

Today it says it is inactive, and it is telling Google that is the finished article.
